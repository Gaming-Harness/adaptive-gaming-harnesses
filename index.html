Knowledge–Probing Co-Evolution for Adaptive Gaming Harnesses

Test-time harness evolution around a frozen gaming VLA

Paper Videos BibTeX
Method Results Videos Knowledge

Abstract

Long-horizon gaming requires an agent to diagnose stalled execution, recover from failures, and reuse that experience on later tasks. We introduce Test-Time Harness Evolution (TTHE), which keeps a gaming vision-language-action model frozen and evolves only the harness around it. A fast loop executes actions. A slow research loop diagnoses failures, proposes harness changes, and checks them in paired sandbox rollouts. Transferable recovery knowledge suggests what to try. Active probes test when that knowledge applies. Validated outcomes then update both future probes and the knowledge bank. On 149 Minecraft tasks spanning mining, combat, and crafting, knowledge-guided evolution covers more tasks than the bare actor and a hand-written fixed harness. The full probing loop improves further over knowledge-only search.

Can recovery knowledge guide what to probe, while probe outcomes reveal which knowledge applies?

Method

The same frozen VLA is shared by Bare, Auto-K, and TTHE. Only the harness changes.

Sand mining with one frozen VLA. Bare executes the actor directly and fails. Auto-K proposes harness edits from a knowledge bank and still fails. TTHE adds probing evidence, accepts a verified edit, and completes the block.

Action probes collect evidence about an ambiguous stall. Knowledge probes test a stored recovery strategy. A vision-language researcher proposes a harness patch, which is kept only when paired sandbox evaluation beats the incumbent. The VLA weights stay fixed.

A fixed knowledge bank is not enough

One rollout on the same 110 Bare failures. Knowledge text stays frozen. No new patch is proposed.

Variant Success Mine Kill Craft
Probe only 15 / 110 5 / 30 6 / 31 4 / 49
Static knowledge, not injected 9 / 110 2 / 30 6 / 31 1 / 49
Static knowledge, no selection update 13 / 110 4 / 30 5 / 31 4 / 49
Static knowledge, adaptive selection 13 / 110 6 / 30 5 / 31 2 / 49

None of the fixed banks beats probing with an empty bank. Injecting a frozen recovery bank and updating which entry is selected solves 13 tasks, against 15 for probing alone.

Limitations

  1. Single backbone. Results use one frozen policy checkpoint in Minecraft. Other actors and games are not tested.
  2. Search cost. Each candidate needs environment rollouts, VLA inference, and researcher calls, plus paired and holdout checks.
  3. Seen tasks. The reported replay covers tasks encountered during adaptation, not a disjoint task set.
  4. Bounded edits. Delayed success credit is heuristic, and the harness can only change the exposed configuration. It cannot exceed what the frozen actor can execute.
